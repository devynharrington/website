I have always been willing to accept a challenge, explore somewhere unfamiliar, and find out what I am capable of accomplishing. That mindset took me through nearly nine and a half years in the United States Marine Corps, into a career at VMware, through the uncertainty of the Broadcom acquisition, and eventually to ClearBridge Technology Group.

When I left the Marine Corps in November 2022, I could not have imagined how quickly life would change. I went from Staff Sergeant and Data Systems Chief to VMware Senior Consultant. A few years later, I was designing VMware Cloud Foundation environments, earning Broadcom Knight recognition across three disciplines, and leading a team of talented federal consultants as a Practice Manager and VCF Architect.

The technical work mattered, and I put everything I had into it. But I did not reach this point alone. ClearBridge gave me continuity when the VMware ecosystem was changing, trusted me with responsibilities that stretched me, recognized initiative, supported my development, and created room for me to discover potential I did not know I had.

## Leadership Started Before the Job Title

My professional foundation was built in the Marine Corps. I served as a Data Systems Chief, spent time as a Marine recruiter, and reached the rank of Staff Sergeant. The work taught me that technical ability is only part of leadership. People need clear expectations, preparation, accountability, and a leader who remains composed when circumstances change.

Romania and Norway reinforced those lessons. During Cold Response in Norway, the winter environment was demanding and unfamiliar. Preparation was not optional, and neither was taking care of the Marines around me. At the same time, the experience opened a part of the world I never expected to see. I explored Norway's landscape and history and stood beneath the Northern Lights. Romania brought Bucharest, military events alongside Romanian partners, and the chance to see Bran Castle.

## From Staff Sergeant to VMware Consultant

In 2022, I entered a six-month VMware Federal SkillBridge program. SkillBridge did not erase my military experience or force me to start from nothing. It translated years of technical leadership, mission focus, and accountability into a new professional environment.

I officially joined VMware as a Senior Consultant in November 2022. My work remained connected to federal missions, but my technical scope expanded from vRealize into Aria, automation, operations, networking, NSX, Kubernetes, vSphere Kubernetes Service, and the broader VMware Cloud Foundation platform.

The transition was exciting, but it also demanded humility. Military leadership did not mean I automatically knew enterprise consulting. I had to learn a new ecosystem, understand customer environments, earn trust again, and become comfortable asking questions. The habits transferred even when the product names and business context did not.

Then the Broadcom acquisition changed the VMware community. Roles, programs, and partner relationships shifted. Like many people, I had understandable questions about what would happen next and whether I could preserve the customer work and momentum I had built.

## ClearBridge Turned Disruption Into Opportunity

I joined ClearBridge Technology Group on May 12, 2025, as a Senior Consultant in its federal VMware practice. The company supported the transfer of my clearance and helped me maintain continuity with meaningful federal and Department of Defense work, including Navy-related projects I already understood. I can only discuss that work at a high level, but the continuity mattered deeply. Moving into the partner ecosystem did not require me to abandon the mission, customers, or technical direction I cared about.

In a little more than a year, I advanced into responsibilities as both a Practice Manager and VCF Architect. That was not simply a new title. ClearBridge's leaders recognized capabilities I was still learning to see in myself. They trusted me with real responsibility, kept me technically engaged, challenged me to think beyond one customer, and gave me opportunities to contribute across the company.

Today, I can architect and support complex VCF environments while leading and mentoring nine highly capable federal consultants. I can help people pursue certifications and enablement, improve how we understand skills and staffing needs, and advocate for consultants as people rather than names assigned to project seats.

I am grateful for the trust and guidance I have received from Joe, Tim Anderson, Practice Director Paula Temple, Larry Wingfield, Jay Rue, and Alan. Each relationship has reinforced something I now believe strongly: leadership development happens through mentorship, honest feedback, and the willingness to let someone carry real responsibility.

ClearBridge did not only ask what I could produce for the company. Its leaders also considered what the company could do to help me grow.

## Meaningful Work Without Sacrificing Family

ClearBridge has allowed me to remain connected to important customer missions, travel when it adds value, and still be present at home when my family needs me. As a husband and father, that flexibility matters tremendously.

Remote work is not about being disconnected. It is about being trusted to perform at a high level while recognizing that employees have lives and responsibilities beyond a billing code. Depending on the engagement, federal consulting can also create opportunities for onsite collaboration and customer-funded travel. The exact arrangements vary by role and project, but my experience has shown me that meaningful customer work, leadership growth, travel, and family presence do not have to be mutually exclusive.

The human connection matters too. ClearBridge flew me to Tampa for a team gathering at Topgolf, covering my round-trip flight, food, and the event before I returned home the next day. It was a short trip, but it let me finally meet coworkers I had previously known through screens, messages, and shared work.

## ClearBridge Gave Me Room to Build

One of the clearest examples of ClearBridge's culture started with a business problem. We needed better visibility into employee skills, certifications, enablement, availability, reporting relationships, and staffing information. A manually maintained process made it difficult for leadership, Delivery, PMO, and resource management to understand the capabilities available across the company and match the right people with customer needs.

I believed we could improve it, so I independently designed and developed an SSO-authenticated skills and resource-management platform. I invested more than 100 hours, including late nights and weekends, while continuing my full-time customer responsibilities. I did not build it because I expected an award. I built it because the problem affected employees, managers, staffing teams, and ultimately our customers.

The platform became a real operational capability. I will not publish its source code, employee information, internal screenshots, or proprietary metrics, but its purpose was straightforward: make skills easier to understand, make staffing decisions more informed, and help talented people find work aligned with their capabilities.

ClearBridge completely surprised me by presenting me with its Q2 2026 Distinguished Achievement Award, the company's highest recognition tier, along with a $1,000 award payment. I was genuinely caught off guard.

## A New Investment in Broadcom Knights

On August 27, 2026, ClearBridge announced its Broadcom Knights Certification Incentive Bonus and Maintenance Allowance Plan for eligible members of the Client Solutions Group. This is a **ClearBridge compensation and development policy**, not a Broadcom corporate compensation program.

According to the supplied policy, eligible employees may receive:

| Investment | Policy provision |
|---|---|
| New Knight achievement | One-time $5,000 incentive payment, subject to plan eligibility |
| Maintaining or revalidating Knight status | Up to $2,500 annually for qualifying home-lab equipment, training, and education |
| VMware Explore | Paid time and eligible expenses |
| Annual recognition | ClearBridge Broadcom Knights swag drop |

{{< alert icon="circle-info" >}}
Eligibility, qualifying expenses, approvals, tax treatment, continued employment requirements, and other terms are governed by the official ClearBridge plan. These benefits describe the policy supplied to me and should not be interpreted as a promise that every employee or candidate will receive identical compensation or opportunities.
{{< /alert >}}

The annual allowance is especially meaningful to me because serious enterprise home labs are expensive. Servers, large memory configurations, NVMe storage, routers, 10 Gb switches, DAC cables, racks, and UPS systems can cost thousands of dollars. Rising component prices make it harder for individuals to create realistic environments on their own.

My VCF 9.1 home lab gives me a safe place to deploy, fail, troubleshoot, rebuild, and learn without risking a customer's production platform. I can practice management-domain deployment, NSX networking, Automation workflows, Virtual Network Appliance services, Supervisor, VKS, upgrades, and recovery. That hands-on repetition builds judgment that no slide deck can replace.

The $2,500 allowance is therefore more than a financial benefit. It lowers the barrier to advanced learning and helps consultants become more prepared and confident in front of customers. The one-time incentive recognizes an achievement; the annual support helps a Knight remain valuable over time.

## Education as a Long-Term Investment

Continuous education has been part of every stage of my growth. I completed an MBA in Leadership with a 4.0 GPA and later earned a Master of Science in Cybersecurity. I have also explored doctoral programs focused on information systems and applied leadership.

No doctorate has been approved, and educational support depends on company policy, alignment, and approval. Still, knowing ClearBridge may support the next level of education makes that possibility feel more attainable. It signals that the company is interested not only in what an employee can deliver today, but also in who that person can become over the next several years.

Being challenged is different from being exploited. The opportunities I have received came with mentorship, recognition, flexibility, trust, and tangible investment. That combination pushed me to grow without making me feel like an interchangeable resource.

## My Responsibility Is to Invest in Others

I am proud of how far I have come since November 2022, but the next chapter cannot be only about me. As a Practice Manager, I have the opportunity to manage, mentor, support, and advocate for nine talented federal consultants.

I want to help them identify development opportunities, sharpen their technical skills, pursue certifications, accept challenges, and reach Broadcom Knight status. I want them to experience the same professional momentum and recognition I have experienced. I also want to make sure their initiative is visible and their goals are heard.

That investment eventually reaches the customer. Current certifications support better architecture decisions. Home labs let consultants encounter failures safely before seeing them in production. Better skills visibility improves staffing alignment. Mentoring multiplies knowledge across a team. People who feel supported bring more confidence, curiosity, and energy to difficult customer problems.
